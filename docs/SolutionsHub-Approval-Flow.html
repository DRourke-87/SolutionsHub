Who approves what, and who we have to appoint

One offering's route from intake form to published entry. Every box is a status in the system; its colour is who the record is waiting on while it sits there. This note exists to settle one question before go-live: how many reviewers, approvers and publishers do we need, and who are they?

01The approval flow

02The five parts to play

Only three of these are assignments an Admin has to make. The other two come free — one with signing in, the other with being named on a record.

Submitter & Owner

Anyone who can sign in may submit. Offering Owners, co-leads and solution architects are named on the record itself and get edit rights to that one record.

No assignment needed

Reviewer

Claims a new submission, works with the owners on the content, sends it back for updates, and marks the review complete. Can be scoped to one or more Business Groups.

1–2 per Business Group

Approver

The approval decision: approve, send back for changes, or reject with a reason. Approving writes an immutable snapshot of the record. Can be scoped to one or more Business Groups.

2 per Business Group

Publisher

Works the readiness checklist, selects the destination, downloads the export package, and records where and when the offering went live. One pool for the whole site.

1–2 in total

Site Admin

Grants and revokes every other role, maintains Business Groups, capabilities and publishing destinations, archives or reopens records, exports the audit log.

2 in total — keep it small

03The two rules that drive the headcount

  1. Nobody completes the review on an offering they are named on.
  2. Nobody approves an offering they are named on — Site Admins included.

Both are enforced in code, not by convention. So a Business Group whose approver is also its most active offering owner will deadlock the first time they submit something: that is why the approver column in the worksheet wants two names, not one. If the business would rather run a single central pool, leave the Business Group scope empty and those reviewers and approvers see everything — scoping can be added later without a code change.

04Every step, and what the system checks first

The right-hand column is enforced server-side on every request. Hiding a button in the interface is a convenience, not the control.

StepWho can do itBlocked unless
Submit Any signed-in user All required fields are filled, 1–3 capabilities chosen, at least one Offering Owner named, and at least one supporting file or resource link attached.
Claim for review Reviewer in scope The record is Submitted.
Request updates Reviewer Approver They have written what needs to change.
Resubmit Recorder, owners, co-leads The record passes the same completeness check as a first submission.
Mark review complete Reviewer in scope The record is complete and no blocking comment is unresolved — and the reviewer is not named on the record.
Approve or reject Approver in scope They are not the recorder, owner or co-lead of that record. A rejection needs a written reason.
Confirm ready to publish Publisher A publishing destination has been selected.
Record publication Publisher The published URL has been entered. The six-month review clock starts here.
Confirm still current Owners and co-leads The record is Published.
